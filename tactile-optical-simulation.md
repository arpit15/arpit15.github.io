We built a fully general optical tactile simulation system for GelSight using physics-based rendering techniques. We propose physically accurate light models and show in-depth analysis of individual components of our simulation pipeline. The technique is fully general and can be extended to simulated sensors of arbitrary geometry, surface material and light sources. Our system outperforms previous simulation techniques qualitatively and quantitatively on image similarity metrics like SSIM.

## Overview

We develop an optical simulation system using physics-based rendering (PBR) techniques. PBR focuses on accurately modeling the physics of light scattering. PBR allows modifying the physical location of optical elements like cameras and lights; changing the deformable surface geometry; changing the optical properties of sensor surfaces (shiny vs diffuse). Therefore, our system can be used as a design tool and simulation software for producing accurate tactile images. Our system is able to successfully capture the variation in color, light intensity and shape at different locations of the elastomer surface.

<figure><img src="{{ img }}Raytracing.jpg" alt="Ray tracing through the sensor"></figure>

We propose specific models of light, translucent "Gelcore" and deformable elastomer surface generation process which allows us to simulate GelSight.

## Experiments

We collected images using our sensor prototype and compare the images generated by our simulation when the object is pressed against the sensor at multiple spatial locations and for arbitrarily complex objects. Please refer to our paper for a complete description of the experiments and to the supplementary material for additional ablation studies.
